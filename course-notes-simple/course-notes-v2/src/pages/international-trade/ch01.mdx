## Case Study: Offer and Acceptance

**Scenario:** A British businessman (A) makes an offer to a German businessman (B) on May 3 to sell certain goods. B receives the offer on May 4 and sends a complete acceptance on the morning of May 4. After sending the offer, A discovers commodity prices have risen and cables B to withdraw the offer on the afternoon of May 5. A receives B's acceptance on the morning of May 6.

**Questions:**
1. Under British law, is it legal for A to withdraw the offer?
2. If the CISG applies, does a contract exist?

### Analysis

> **British Law:** It is **illegal** for A to withdraw. Once acceptance is sent, it is effective immediately. B made a promise on May 4 morning, so the contract already existed before A's withdrawal.

> **CISG:** Both parties **do have** a contract. An offer can be withdrawn only before the offeree dispatches acceptance. Since B already committed before A's withdrawal advice, A cannot withdraw.

## Section 1: Definition and Role of International Trade

**International trade** is the worldwide exchange of goods and services among countries.

### Reasons for International Trade
- Resource endowment differences between countries
- Comparative advantage and specialization
- Economies of scale
- Access to larger markets

### Role of International Trade
- Promotes economic growth and development
- Facilitates technology transfer
- Creates employment opportunities
- Enhances consumer choice

## Section 2: Differences — International vs Domestic Trade

| Aspect | Domestic Trade | International Trade |
|--------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Currency** | Single currency | Multiple foreign currencies |
| **Culture** | Same language and culture | Different languages and cultures |
| **Regulations** | Uniform national laws | Varying rules across countries |
| **Risk Level** | Lower | Higher (political, credit, transport) |
| **Communication** | Straightforward | More complex control systems |
| **Documentation** | Minimal | Extensive (customs, shipping, banking) |

## Section 3: Laws and Practices

### Application of the Law

**China Contract Law Article 126:** Parties to a contract involving foreign interests **can choose the applicable law** to process contract disputes. If no choice is made, the law of the country with the **closest connection** shall apply.

### Key International Practices

| Practice | Full Name | Purpose |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| **UCP 600** | Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits, ICC Pub. No. 600 | Governs letter of credit operations |
| **INCOTERMS** | International Rules for the Interpretation of Trade Terms | Defines obligations of buyers and sellers |

### Nature of International Trade Customs

1. **Not compulsory** — Not legislation for all countries
2. **Binding when cited** — When both parties cite a custom, it becomes legally valid
3. **Implied binding** — Even without explicit indication, customs may have binding force
4. **Contract prevails** — If contract clauses conflict with customs, the contract is followed
5. **Modifiable** — Parties can make clauses different from customs when signing

## Section 4: Export Procedures (L/C + CIF)

### Stage 1: Preparation
1. Selection of the right market
2. Finding potential partners
3. Studying customer creditability
4. Applying for export license

### Stage 2: Negotiation
```
Enquiry → Offer → Counter offer → Acceptance → Sales Contract
```

### Stage 3: Performance

| Step | Action | Party |
|------|--------|-------|
| 1 | Getting goods ready | Exporter |
| 2 | Ensure issuance of L/C | Importer/Bank |
| 3 | Booking shipping space | Exporter/Forwarder |
| 4 | Obtaining insurance | Exporter (CIF) |
| 5 | Applying for inspection | Exporter |
| 6 | Clearing goods for export | Customs/Exporter |
| 7 | Loading and getting B/L | Shipping line |
| 8 | Presenting documents for bank negotiation | Exporter |

> **Bill of Lading (B/L)** is the most critical document — serves as receipt, contract evidence, and document of title.

### Stage 4: Disputes Settlement
- Friendly negotiation
- Mediation
- Arbitration
- Litigation

---

## Summary

International trade procedure consists of:
1. **Preparation** of a transaction
2. **Negotiation** of the contract
3. **Signing** the contract
4. **Performance** of the contract
5. **Settlement of disputes** (if applicable)

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Trade terms** | Standardized expressions defining buyer/seller obligations |
| **Trade customs** | Widely accepted practices in international commerce |
| **Import trade** | Purchase of foreign goods/services |
| **Export trade** | Sale of domestic goods/services abroad |
| **Sales contract** | Legal agreement between buyer and seller |
| **Export procedure** | Steps required to ship goods out of a country |
| **Import procedure** | Steps required to bring goods into a country |
